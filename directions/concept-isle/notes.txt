Crystal Isle, path traced

What this is
  Dawn over still water in a pink and violet mist. A quiet island of terraced gardens, scanned trees
  and a few warm-windowed stone houses rises from the water; on its crown, on a low dark plinth, stands
  the Antimony mark as a monument: a dark stone tile (100 by 100 with corners of radius 22, 76 m square,
  about 9 m deep) split top to bottom by the seam at 16 degrees, with the sky crystal set in its
  cavity. Every outline is the mark's exact geometry from break-geometry.json: the tile, the cavity
  (cut as a recess, so dark stone stays behind the crystal), the seam (cut clean through, so the sky
  shows in it) and the gem, whose two front facets meet on the seam. The crystal is clear glass tinted
  #94E6FF with a smaller gem of sky light inside it. The low sun sits behind a ridge of trees on an
  islet beyond, and the mist hugs the water.
  Camera: 28 mm on 36 mm, 326 m from the mark, 6 m above the water, 2.5 degrees of tilt, f/8. The
  mark is at the exact horizontal centre of the frame, so the tall phone crop keeps it whole.

  Blender 4.5.15 LTS, Cycles on CPU (4 threads), OpenImageDenoise, adaptive sampling, AgX Base
  Contrast. Sky: Poly Haven kloppenheim_06_puresky, desaturated and held under a dawn gradient with
  a broad glow about the sun. Scans: Poly Haven trees, rocks, grass and cliff; ambientCG stone and
  grass (see credits.txt).

How the crystal is held to sky blue
  - Light linking: the light inside the crystal lights the stone, island, trees and water but never
    the mist, so it cannot veil itself in white.
  - A blue absorption volume inside the glass, so backlight turns blue as it passes through.
  - An object-masked grade in the compositor (object index pass): the crystal's pixels are pulled
    to #94E6FF before the bloom.

Run
  blender -b -P crystal_isle.py -- --out beauty.png --masks masks.png --w 2560 --h 1440 --samples 256 --tl 1080
    (always under the shared lock). One run writes the graded still and, after it, a flat mask render
    from the same camera: R the crystal and its reflection, G the grass, B the water.
  python3 isle_compose.py --still beauty.png --masks masks.png --out assets
    grades the still (violet shadows, a darker lower frame, a soft vignette), writes the stills
    (PNG, WebP, AVIF, wide and phone) and composes the loop.

The loop (6 s, 24 fps, 144 frames, seamless), composed from the one still and its masks
  - the crystal's light breathes, about 7 percent, through a soft blur of its mask (one cycle)
  - the water: reflections waver by a pixel or two, more near the camera (two and three cycles)
  - the grass sways a little (two cycles)
  - the mist breathes: two soft noise fields crossfade over the loop, low about the waterline
  - fine grain, new on every frame
  Every motion divides 144 frames, so frame 144 runs straight into frame 1.

Page (index.html)
  The still (AVIF, WebP), then the loop over it (WebM or H.264; a tall cut on phones). A short
  opening, once a session: the frame rises out of ink while the mist lifts off the mark, then the
  type arrives; a touch, key or scroll skips it. The wordmark and "Software, made on purpose."
  bottom left, Orbe, immiscible and Idyll bottom right, one sound toggle top right. Sound is made
  in the browser and silent until asked: a low warm air, a slow lap of water at the stones, wind in
  the grass, and now and then a far glass chime. Reduced motion: no opening, no loop, the still.

Honest limits
  - The loop is made from one still: the water's motion is an image warp, not rendered ripples, and
    the mist does not truly drift.
  - The light shafts are faint; a final pass would add a stronger break-up close behind the island.
