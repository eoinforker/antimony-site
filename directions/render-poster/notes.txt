Poster 1, path traced

What this is
  poster.py builds the Poster 1 frame procedurally and renders it with Cycles: one board-marked
  precast concrete wall (1.8 by 0.9 panels, tie holes, runs below them, rising damp), a slim dark
  lightbox (1.16 by 1.74, foot 0.17 above the pavement) behind wet glass with drops on it, the
  downpipe and a clipped cable, big wet pavement slabs with standing water and rain rings, rain
  streaks, light haze. Light comes from the print itself, a cool spot lamp high off frame to the
  left and a faint cobalt glow off frame to the right. The camera matches the real-time framing.
  The only image is the print: Antimony's Grow page (directions/grow/shots/rest-desktop.png)
  cropped to a portrait sheet, with the Break mark redrawn from its exact geometry (the Grow shot
  has a blade of grass at the foot of the seam; the redraw removes it, so nothing grows on the mark).

  Run: blender -b -P poster.py -- --variant night       (or overgrown)
  Blender 4.5.15 LTS, Cycles on CPU, OpenImageDenoise, adaptive sampling, AgX (Base Contrast).

Render times, 1920 by 1200, 4 CPU threads, no GPU
  night.png      674 s  (11 min 14 s), adaptive sampling converged at the 400 sample cap
  overgrown.png  1395 s (23 min 15 s), stopped by the 23 minute time limit at about 230 samples
  The machine was shared with other jobs for part of the session, so treat these as upper bounds.

Overgrown, and one honest limit
  Ivy climbs from the pavement and parts round the frame (the climber turns away from the box;
  no leaf is allowed over the print), moss cushions sit in the tie holes, grass and ferns at the
  wall's foot and in the slab joints, small white and pale sky flowers in the cracks, soft
  daylight from an open sky with a low sun from the left, the lightbox still lit.
  Puddles full of sky: with this frontal, slightly downward framing, every puddle in frame
  reflects the lower wall, never the sky (the reflected rays hit the wall within about 0.6 m of
  the pavement). The puddles therefore mirror the daylit wall, the ivy and the print. A lower
  camera, or a frame that shows the wall's top edge, would be needed to put sky in them.
