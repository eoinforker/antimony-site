Blade Runner, path traced

The scene
  A high room at night, some 260 m above the street. Three tall bays of glass between concrete
  fins, slatted blinds let down to different heights, a low stone table with a drum lamp (the one
  warm practical), a polished dark floor. Out across the void the city falls away into a canyon of
  lit windows and haze; to the right the Antimony tower, a battered brutalist slab with a few warm
  slots, carries the Break on its crown: the stone tile as a panel proud of the face, the seam and
  the cavity cut through to a black recess, the crystal (exact geometry) the one clean cold light.
  To the left, far off and veiled by the haze, a building sized screen shows the meadow poster
  (a clean frame exported from directions/poster). Cold light from an unseen advertising wall falls
  in across the floor with the fins' shadows. Every twenty seconds an unseen spinner hangs above the
  window and its searchlight rakes through the blinds, the haze and across the floor.

  Blender 4.5.15 LTS, Cycles on CPU (4 threads), OpenImageDenoise with albedo and normal,
  adaptive sampling, light tree; the AgX view applied in compose.py through OpenColorIO.

  Cheats, as on a film set
  - Haze is seen, not bounced through: only camera rays scatter in it.
  - The city's lit windows are not sampled as lights (they glow, they do not light); their glow
    in the haze comes from a few big area lights at street level (mostly cold, two sodium).
  - Every area light is invisible to the camera and to reflections.
  - Rain mist close to the glass exists only in the searchlight pass, so the beam has something
    to show up in while the rest frame stays clear.

Passes and the loop (20 s at 24 fps, 480 frames, seamless)
  scene.py --pass full     everything at rest, with haze            1920 x 1200
  scene.py --pass nohaze   the same without haze                    1920 x 1200
  scene.py --pass flick    only the two failing advertising walls   1920 x 1200
  scene.py --pass sweep    only the searchlight, every other frame   960 x 600, frames 1 to 108
  compose.py then builds each frame from linear light:
    nohaze + min(h, 0) + drift(x, y, t) * max(h, 0), h = full - nohaze: the haze's glow drifting left
    + (v(t) - 1) * flick                         the ad walls stutter and brown out
    + sweep(t)                                   frames 265 to 323 (11.0 s to 13.4 s), lamp out from 12.9 s
  with the in between searchlight frames as the mean of their neighbours, a soft bloom, a
  vignette, AgX, then straight into ffmpeg (H.264 and VP9) with no frames on disk.
  The drift fields are periodic noise scrolled by whole periods, so frame 480 runs into frame 1.

Render times (4 slow cores shared with other jobs; every render inside the shared lock)
  full     1920 x 1200, 256 samples cap, stopped by its 19 min limit   1178 s render (26.5 min wall with lock waits)
  nohaze   1920 x 1200, 160 samples                                    663 s
  flick    1920 x 1200, 96 samples                                     448 s
  sweep    960 x 600, 16 samples, frames 1 to 59 odd (30 frames)        about 32 s each, 16 min
  compose  480 frames, bloom, AgX, H.264 and VP9 in one pass           12 min
  Cut short at Eóin's request for speed: the searchlight frames 61 to 108 were never rendered, so
  compose.py fades the lamp out over frames 46 to 59 (12.9 s to 13.4 s) instead of letting the
  spinner carry it across the room and away. A full re-render would add those 48 frames (about
  26 min), all 108 at 1920 x 1200 and 32 samples (about 3 hours), and a longer full pass.

The live layer (index.html, WebGL over the film, composited in frame coordinates)
  Rain on the glass: beads that gather and evaporate, and drops that let go and run with broken
  trails, each a small lens showing the city refracted and inverted; only on clear glass below the
  blinds, never over the lamp. Rain beyond the glass in two depths, backlit by
  the city and sliding with the pointer. Condensation low on the cold panes that the pointer
  wipes and that slowly fogs back. When the searchlight crosses the glass its water glints, in
  sync with the film. Film grain.
