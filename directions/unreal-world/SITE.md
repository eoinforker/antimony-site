# The website (the end goal), in the owner's words, 28 Oct 2026

Antimony is a software company; the Unreal world exists to make its website.

## What a visitor gets

- **The opening:** a full-screen, living scene. A hillside of glowing blue flowers under one enormous luminous form
  (our own shape, from the crystal or the mark, not a game's tree), light breaking through mist in rays. It moves
  gently: drifting mist, swaying flowers, shifting light. The view follows the mouse or the phone's tilt.
- **The poster in the world:** the cobalt Antimony print stands in the field, lit from within, the one made thing in
  the landscape. Over it, the hero line: "Large things, made with intention, for the people who make the work."
- **More scenes:** visitors move between a few worlds (the coast, the starlit field, later perhaps a machine-city in
  the FF7 style). Each has its own poster line: "Made light." / "Quietly, a very large thing." and the rest.
- **Walk-in moment (the hero scene):** a 3D version you can look around and drift a few metres through.
- **Chip the stone:** visitors chip pieces off the stone to reveal the products, one per chip: Orbe, immiscible,
  Idyll, "Something else is growing.", "Antimony. More, slowly."

## The bar

- Elden Ring for nature, light and air; FF7 Remake for built detail. The look is ours, not a copy.
- Magical, and crafted well enough that designers and creative VCs respect it: a systems lab that means to become a
  goliath, saying so calmly.
- Fast to load: a light first view, with the heavy parts loading only when wanted.
- Rendered as high resolution as the GPU allows.

## How it gets made

Unreal renders the worlds on the rented GPU: stills, seamless loops and depth layers, plus the 3D capture of the hero
scene. The site itself is plain HTML with WebGL on top. Unreal never runs in the visitor's browser.
