Lens, path traced

What this is
  lens_scene.py builds the Break as an object and renders it with Cycles: a 20 cm tile of dark honed
  stone, 4 cm thick, wet from rain (a water film over most of the face, drier honed patches, about
  seventy beads), the seam cut right through it, the cavity a seat 5 mm deep, the crystal a thin
  faceted lens set flush in a hole its own shape. Every outline is the mark's exact geometry from
  assets/break-geometry.json. It stands on a wet parapet; the city is far behind it and below, out
  of focus; a lamp behind it to the right, a cobalt rim from behind on the left, the street behind the
  camera in its reflections. 85 mm lens, f/5.6 (desktop) or f/10 (phone), focused on the crystal.

  Four passes from one fixed camera, all linear EXR:
    beauty  the night scene; the openings read black to the camera and to the glass
    thru    how much light reaches the backplane behind the stone (white backplane, all else black
            but still mirror-like, so wet reflections of the openings are counted too)
    uv      the direction each ray leaves the stone, as camera tangents (Window-free, so the meadow
            can be "at infinity" and the plane can sit right behind the stone)
    spill   the daylight let out: a warm area light behind the stone and an emissive crystal hidden
            from the camera, plus a thin mist in front that only camera rays scatter in
  The page adds light linearly: beauty + thru x meadow(uv + tilt) + k x spill, then AgX.

  meadow.py renders the world inside: a painted gradient sky with real volume cumulus lit by a sun
  lamp, and about 280,000 grass blades (plus tall foreground stems and a few pale flowers) on rolling
  ground with aerial haze. Two layers (sky, grass with alpha) so the wind can move the grass on the
  page. Its camera is a pinhole linear in view tangent: tx in [-0.62, 0.62], ty in [-0.30, 0.54].

  build_assets.py encodes the passes for the page (light as log2(1 + 256x) / 13 in 8 bits; tangents as
  12 bits each packed in an RGB PNG, read nearest and interpolated by hand) and composes rest.webp,
  the still shown before WebGL is ready and instead of it.

Render times, 4 shared CPU threads, no GPU (each run waited for the shared lock first)
  meadow sky    2048 x 1388, 64 samples, volumes     about 13 min (time limit 15)
  meadow grass  2048 x 1388, 64 samples              about 10 min
  desktop beauty 1920 x 1200, up to 512 samples      18 min 36 s (stopped by the 18 min 20 s limit)
  desktop thru   1920 x 1200, 128 samples            4 min 30 s
  desktop uv     1920 x 1200, 96 samples, no denoise 4 min
  desktop spill  1920 x 1200, 192 samples, mist      about 12 min
  phone beauty   1000 x 2160, up to 384 samples      13 min 21 s; thru about 5 min, uv about 5 min
  phone spill    450 x 972, 64 samples (cut short for time, upscaled; the light is soft)  2 min 28 s

Honest limits
  The clouds are clustered soft volumes: good at the size they are seen through the crystal, a little
  cotton-like if you look at meadow_sky on its own. The spill is light from behind and inside the
  crystal; its caustics are approximate (the emissive crystal is a film-set practical). The tilt moves
  the view through the glass as the real geometry bends it for the untilted stone, plus a first-order
  swing; it is not a re-render of a tilted stone, so the stone's own reflections do not move.
