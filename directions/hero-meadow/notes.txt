Hero Meadow, path traced (finished after the push; Eóin approved the second frame)

What this is
  The Antimony stone (the shared module, directions/stone/stone.py: exact Break geometry, dark honed
  basalt, softened and chipped arrises, shallow tooling, a damp foot; nothing grows on the mark) standing
  on a low rise in a summer meadow in the low, warm light of late afternoon. Beyond it the field falls into
  a shallow dip and rises to a far ridge with a hedgerow of scanned trees; past that the land layers away
  in haze to a great escarpment, a long pale edge of rock that rises off the right of frame and recedes
  15 km towards the sun. Above is a wide sky of sculpted cumulus.
  Camera: 45 mm on 36 mm, 1.9 m high, 12.5 m from the stone, pitched up 3.5 degrees, f/5.6 (so the far
  land holds), focused on the stone. Sun 34 degrees left of the view and 4.6 degrees up.
  The stone is 2.1 m, 0.34 m thick, turned 15 degrees to the camera so its arris and side show while the
  seam still reads; a warm bounce (an area light linked to the stone alone) gives the honed face weight.
  Blender 4.5.15 LTS, Cycles on CPU (4 threads), OpenImageDenoise, adaptive sampling, light tree,
  AgX, with a soft bloom in the compositor.

Assets: see credits.txt (Poly Haven and ambientCG, all CC0).

Light
  The puresky HDRI lights the scene (4k), and camera rays see its 8k version, so the clouds stay
  sharp. Its sun is softened by haze, so a matched sun lamp (warm, 0.6 degrees) at the HDRI's sun
  direction gives the raking light and long shadows. Refractive caustics from that sun go through
  the crystal (MNEE, set up by the stone module).
  Cloud shadows: a gobo plane 450 m up, seen only by shadow rays, which stops only rays travelling within
  a degree or two of the sun; so it lays cumulus shadows on the land from the low sun without dimming the
  sky light. It is kept clear over the stone and the near meadow.

The far escarpment
  Poly Haven coastal_cliff_01 and 02 (scanned cliff bands, 92 m and 41 m long) scaled up about 40 times,
  as a matte painter would, and set end to end as one long edge turned 20 degrees towards the sun, so its
  face and ledges catch the light. A nearer, lower spur breaks down into the land. The scan's colour is
  calmed towards one even rock (at kilometres its blotches read as cloud), and the rock and the nearer hills
  carry their own aerial perspective in the shader: the sky's colour (from a 64 by 32 copy, so no cloud is
  painted onto the rock) by distance, capped so the rock stays opaque, with a low mist rising only from its
  base. The data pass marks them B = 0.25, and the grade hazes them only lightly.

The paper
  A folded letter, 30 by 21 cm, rests in the short grass at the stone's front left foot, in the low sun,
  with a small clearing round it so the grass never buries it (it used to sit in the seam, where it read
  as a sticker). It is a real object in the render, with the same camera, light and shadow. Its frame
  position is exported (<out>.paper.json) and placed over the picture as a focusable button ("Read the
  note"). Opening it eases the view in towards the paper; the note then lifts and settles flat, showing
  the approved words on ambientCG Paper002, lit to match the scene. Escape, the close mark or a click
  outside eases back. After a quiet spell the paper catches the light twice.
  The letter carries the approved copy (rules.md); its side margins are 12 percent of the card's own width
  and the type scales with the viewport's width as well as its height, so the longer paragraph sets as one
  calm block on desktop and on a 390 px phone.
  Other interaction: a few pixels of pointer parallax; with sound on, the wind swells as the pointer
  moves and when the note is opened.

Grade windows (meadow_post.py), as a colourist would
  - The crystal's colour is pulled onto #94E6FF (0.5), keeping its light, so it reads as pale sky glass.
  - The grey undersides of the clouds are warmed (peach, never amber).
  - Aerial perspective: distance takes on the colour of the sky just above it, from the depth pass.
  Then lens distortion, lateral colour, halation, a vignette and grain.

Run (S is the scratchpad; the shared arguments are in S/hmp/draft.sh, the scene's in S/hmp/final3_args.txt)
  blender -b -P meadow_scene.py -- --assets <grass, rock> --acg <assets/ambientcg> --ph <assets/polyhaven>
    --stone directions/stone/stone.py --hdri <kloppenheim_06_puresky_4k.hdr> --sky <..._8k.hdr>
    --sun-u 0.612 --sun-el 4.6 --sun-str 3 --mound 0.75 --density 1.0 --lens 45 --pitch 3.5 --cam-h 1.9 --my 12.5 --stone-m 2.1 --thick 0.34 --yaw 15 --moss 0 --clean-mark 1 --carve 0.25 --damp-foot 0.4 --chip-size 1.4 --stone-value 1.1 --bounce 25 --paper-at foot --paper-dx -1.05 --paper-dy 2.7 --paper-lift 0.11 --paper-w 0.30 --paper-l 0.21 --paper-clear 0.42 --fstop 5.6 --clouds 0.9 --cloud-lo 0.42 --cloud-clear 40 --wind 0.18 --sun-az 34 --escarp 1 --escarp-h 460 --esc-near-r 3000 --esc-near-a 27 --escarp-turn 20 --spur 1 --air-k 9000 --air-max 0.5 --mist 0.45 --air-lift 0.01 --esc-calm 0.6
    --res 2560x1440 --samples 1024 --time 1800 --out day.png --passes day_data.png

  Stills, loops and encodes: S/hmp/build.sh (python3 meadow_post.py still ... --crystal 0.5; loop ... --drift).

The loop (8 s, 24 fps, 192 frames, seamless), built from the one still and its data pass
  The data pass is a 16-bit PNG: R log depth (ln(1 + d) / ln(4001)), G blade height fraction,
  B mask (crystal 1, stone and paper 0.75, grass and flowers 0.5, far land 0.25, sky 0).
  - Grass sway: a displacement field from three travelling waves, scaled by blade height (tips
    move most) and by nearness; the gust crests show as a faint travelling sheen in the grass.
    The stone, crystal and paper are held still.
  - Cloud shadows drift over the land and the escarpment (from 60 m out; the stone and the near meadow
    stay in the sun), on ground coordinates rebuilt from the depth pass.
  - The clouds drift a few pixels (sky filled behind the land first, so nothing smears).
  - Light breathing: about 3 percent, more in the highlights. A few warm pollen motes, fewer than before.
  The two drifts close the loop by crossfading two offset copies; the offsets are small against the
  pattern, so no doubling shows. Every other motion repeats a whole number of times in 8 s.

Honest limits
  - The meadow flowers are modelled in the script; Poly Haven's scanned flowers are dry-country
    species that would not belong here.
  - The sway is a warp of the still, not a re-render; the far hedgerow does not stir.
  - The escarpment is scanned cliff at many times its size: its form holds in haze, but its texture
    is soft (1k maps over kilometres) and its face reads more as pale chalk than as a named rock.
  - The hedgerow trees are small scanned trees scaled up; at 300 m and in haze they read well.

Bloom (the night variant, night-desktop.png and night-phone.png) is shelved; its page and loops
