The Garden in the Ruin, path traced

What this is
  Inside a vast old stone hall at day. The nave roof is broken open in two places: one ragged break lets in a single
  steep shaft of warm sun, the other, far down the nave, shows the sky at the top of the frame. The shaft falls
  through drifting dust onto a bed of flowers growing where the flagstones have gone. The Antimony stone (2.6 m) stands
  in the light among them, its sky crystal lit. A shallow sheet of still water in the floor holds the stone, the
  crystal and the far window. Arcades of round arches on columns recede into cool shade; ivy climbs and hangs from
  the columns, ferns grow at their feet, fallen roof blocks and a column drum lie in the aisles. A folded letter
  rests on a fallen block at the edge of the light.
  Camera: 22 mm on 36 mm, 0.95 m high, 9 m from the stone, pitched up 9 degrees so the broken roof shows, f/3.2.
  Blender 4.5.15 LTS, Cycles on CPU, OpenImageDenoise, adaptive sampling, light tree, AgX Medium High Contrast.

Everything is scanned (credits.txt): Poly Haven flagstones, ashlar and plaster; ambientCG moss, loam and ivy
leaves; Poly Haven heliophila (white, and a copy tinted pale blue), celandine (yellow), grass, ferns and mossy
rubble. The stone is the shared directions/stone/stone.py (exact Break geometry, honed basalt, the crystal as glass).
The architecture is invented, kept simple and big (no scan of a hall exists), and stays mostly in shade.

The six fixes after the first frame
  1. The stone is lit so the whole seam reads: the shaft lands on it, and a warm bounce card (the light the pool
     of sunlit flowers would give back) lights its face; the stone's value is held dark so the cut reads.
  2. Nothing tall stands before the stone: in a lane in front of it there are only small flowers and tiny tufts.
     The camera came closer (9 m, from 16) and lower (0.95 m).
  3. A tighter shaft (1.7 m break, sun 0.9 degrees wide) aimed at the stone, with denser air only along the beam,
     so it has an edge and lands as a pool.
  4. More colour in the flowers, and a drift of white and blue flowers near the lens at the lower left. Warm sun
     turns pale blue petals lilac, so the grade holds them to a pale sky blue (flower hues only).
  5. The far window is narrower (4.4 m), lower and further into the haze, and the camera turns so it sits well left
     of the stone.
  6. A large cool skylight high in the nave (unseen) lifts the columns, arches and ivy out of black; the camera
     pitches up to show the far break in the roof.

Cheats, all common on film sets
  - A warm bounce card and a cool skylight panel, invisible to the camera and to reflections.
  - Denser air along the shaft (a volume column), so the light has shape without fogging the whole hall.
  - Refractive caustics are off; the crystal's own faint glow stands in for the sun through it.
  - The grade pulls lilac petals back to pale blue.

Run (S is the scratchpad), always under the shared lock
  flock $S/blender.lock nice -n 5 blender -b --factory-startup -P garden_scene.py -- --ph $S/assets/polyhaven \
    --acg $S/assets/ambientcg --out beauty.png --passes data.png --res 2560x1440 --samples 400 --time 1100
  python3 -I garden_post.py still beauty.png beauty.png.json assets      (graded stills, phone crop, AVIF and WebP)
  python3 -I garden_post.py depth data.png assets/garden-depth.png --w 1280 --json beauty.png.json
  python3 -I garden_post.py depth data.png assets/garden-depth-phone.png --w 720 --crop phone --json beauty.png.json
  python3 -I garden_post.py loop beauty.png data.png beauty.png.json <frames> --w 1920            (and --w 720 --crop phone)
  ffmpeg: H.264 CRF 20 (slow, faststart), VP9 CRF 33.
  python3 -I build_page.py beauty.png.json assets/wordmark.svg      (index.html from page.src.html, with the layout)
  Final render: 2560x1440, 400 samples capped at 1100 s: 1123 s of rendering.

The loop (8 s, 24 fps, 192 frames, seamless), built from the one still and its data pass
  The data pass: R depth / 60 m, G still water, B object (stone, crystal, letter). The beam is not a pass: it is
  worked out from the camera and the sun (beauty.png.json), as the part of each view ray inside the shaft.
  - Motes: 240 specks of dust in the beam, each on its own closed path, lit by the beam, hidden behind nearer
    things, soft with the lens's depth of field.
  - Light breathing: the shaft and its pool swell and settle by about 5 percent, once per loop.
  - Water: a slow shimmer, and two drips from the roof setting rings across the water (one once a loop, one twice).
  The stone and crystal never move. Every motion repeats a whole number of times in 8 s.
  Sizes: desktop H.264 0.7 MB, WebM 0.3 MB; phone H.264 0.5 MB, WebM 0.15 MB.

The page (index.html)
  The loop is the background (WebM or H.264), with the AVIF or WebP still as its poster and fallback. A small WebGL
  layer draws the loop through its depth map, pivoted on the stone, so the stone holds still while the near flowers
  and water come forward and the far hall falls back: a slow breath over about a minute and a gentle answer to the
  pointer or tilt. Opening: the hall comes up out of black over about 3.5 s, then the type; any click, key, scroll
  or touch skips it; once per session. Type: the wordmark and "Software, made on purpose." (alternatives in a
  comment), small, lower left. No products, no links.
  The paper: the folded letter on the fallen block is real geometry in the render. A focusable button ("Read the
  note") sits over it; after about seven quiet seconds a soft glint passes over it. Opening it eases the hall in
  towards the letter, the sheet lifts from the block with a turn and settles flat, lit as the hall is (warm from high
  on the left, cool shade on the right) with the fold across its middle and ambientCG Paper002 grain. Escape, the
  close mark or a click outside eases back. Reduced motion: a crossfade, and the still instead of the loop.
  Sound (procedural, muted until the toggle): the hall's air (low filtered noise through a long synthesised
  reverb), birds outside heard through the windows (mostly reverb), and the loop's own two drips into the water,
  timed to the rings. The air swells a little as the pointer moves; the hall goes muffled while the letter is open.
  Phones: the tall crop keeps the stone whole and the letter in view; on very narrow screens the frame is scaled to
  keep both, anchored at the floor, and the dark vault fades in above.
  Checked with Playwright (Chromium, SwiftShader) at 1440x900 and 390x844: no console errors, no overflow; the
  letter opens and closes. Shots in shots/.

Honest limits
  - The letter is small in the frame (an A4 sheet at 8.6 m): its button is padded and the glint finds it, but by eye
    it is a pale sliver on the block.
  - The haze is still a little milky on the upper left, where the far break's sun crosses the nave.
  - The flowers in front of the stone are kept very low, so the bed reads thinner there than at its sides.
  - On the narrowest phones the vault fades to black at the top rather than showing the roof.
  - The motes and ripples are image effects over the still: the drips do not splash and the dust casts no light.
