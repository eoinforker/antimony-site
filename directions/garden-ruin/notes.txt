The Garden in the Ruin, path traced

What this is
  Inside a vast old stone hall at day. The nave roof is broken open in two places: one ragged break lets in a single
  steep shaft of warm sun, the other, far down the nave, shows the sky at the top of the frame. The shaft falls
  through drifting dust onto a bed of flowers growing where the flagstones have gone. The Antimony stone (2.6 m),
  hand-carved dark basalt, stands in the light among them, its crystal clear sky glass. A shallow sheet of still
  water in the floor holds the stone, the crystal and the far arch. At the far end the great window has lost its
  sill and glazing and opens as an arch into a second hall, roofless and sunlit, its arcade broken down towards a
  far gable with a great open arch, with low ridges beyond in haze. Arcades of round arches on columns recede into
  cool shade; ivy climbs and hangs from the columns, ferns grow at their feet, fallen roof blocks and a column drum
  lie in the aisles. A folded letter leans against a fallen block at the edge of the light.
  Camera: 22 mm on 36 mm, 0.95 m high, 9 m from the stone, pitched up 9 degrees so the broken roof shows, f/3.2.
  Blender 4.5.15 LTS, Cycles on CPU, OpenImageDenoise, adaptive sampling, light tree, AgX Medium High Contrast.

Everything is scanned (credits.txt): Poly Haven flagstones, ashlar and plaster; ambientCG moss, loam and ivy
leaves; Poly Haven heliophila (white, and a copy tinted pale blue), celandine (yellow), grass, ferns and mossy
rubble. The stone is the shared directions/stone/stone.py (exact Break geometry, honed basalt, the crystal as glass).
The architecture is invented, kept simple and big (no scan of a hall exists), and stays mostly in shade.

The six fixes after the first frame
  1. The stone is lit so the whole seam reads: the shaft lands on it, and a warm bounce card (the light the pool
     of sunlit flowers would give back) lights its face; the stone's value is held dark so the cut reads.
  2. Nothing tall stands before the stone: in a lane in front of it there are only small flowers and tiny tufts.
     The camera came closer (9 m, from 16) and lower (0.95 m).
  3. A tighter shaft (1.7 m break, sun 0.9 degrees wide) aimed at the stone, with denser air only along the beam,
     so it has an edge and lands as a pool.
  4. More colour in the flowers, and a drift of white and blue flowers near the lens at the lower left. Warm sun
     turns pale blue petals lilac, so the grade holds them to a pale sky blue (flower hues only).
  5. The far window is narrower (4.4 m), lower and further into the haze, and the camera turns so it sits well left
     of the stone.
  6. A large cool skylight high in the nave (unseen) lifts the columns, arches and ivy out of black; the camera
     pitches up to show the far break in the roof.

Cheats, all common on film sets
  - A warm bounce card and a cool skylight panel, invisible to the camera and to reflections.
  - Denser air along the shaft (a volume column), so the light has shape without fogging the whole hall.
  - A soft card behind the stone, seen only through the crystal (invisible to the lens, lights nothing), so the glass
    has light to transmit, as in a gem photograph. The crystal has no glow of its own.
  - The beam's dust thins low down, so it does not veil the far arch where the line of sight crosses it.
  - The far ridges are a step darker than the sunlit scan, so they hold against the sky.
  - The grade pulls lilac petals back to pale blue.

Run (S is the scratchpad), always under the shared lock
  flock $S/blender.lock nice -n 5 blender -b --factory-startup -P garden_scene.py -- --ph $S/assets/polyhaven \
    --acg $S/assets/ambientcg --out beauty.png --passes data.png --res 2560x1440 --samples 400 --time 2700
  (writes beauty.png, beauty.png.json, data.png (16-bit, Raw) and data.npz: the same data, linear and exact)
  python3 -I garden_post.py still beauty.png beauty.png.json <outdir>     (graded stills, phone crop, AVIF and WebP)
  python3 -I garden_post.py depth data.npz assets/garden-depth.webp --w 1280 --json beauty.png.json
  python3 -I garden_post.py depth data.npz assets/garden-depth-phone.webp --w 720 --crop phone --json beauty.png.json
  python3 -I garden_post.py loop beauty.png data.npz beauty.png.json <frames> --w 1920            (and --w 720 --crop phone)
  ffmpeg: H.264 CRF 20 (slow, faststart), VP9 CRF 33.
  python3 -I build_page.py assets/render.json assets/wordmark.svg      (index.html from page.src.html, with the layout)
  Final render: 2560x1440, 400 samples capped at 2700 s: 2720 s of rendering (45 min).

The loop (8 s, 24 fps, 192 frames, seamless), built from the one still and its data pass
  The data pass: R depth / 60 m, G still water, B object (stone, crystal, letter). The beam is not a pass: it is
  worked out from the camera and the sun (beauty.png.json), as the part of each view ray inside the shaft.
  - Motes: 240 specks of dust in the beam, each on its own closed path, lit by the beam, hidden behind nearer
    things, soft with the lens's depth of field.
  - Light breathing: the shaft and its pool swell and settle by about 5 percent, once per loop.
  - Water: a slow shimmer, and two drips from the roof setting rings across the water (one once a loop, one twice).
  - Petals: six, each falling once a loop from high in the shaft to the flowers, swaying and turning so they catch
    the sun, fading in high and out low, lit only inside the beam.
  The stone and crystal never move. Every motion repeats a whole number of times in 8 s.
  Sizes: desktop H.264 0.78 MB, WebM 0.32 MB; phone H.264 0.47 MB, WebM 0.16 MB. Stills: AVIF 84 KB (phone 55 KB),
  WebP 116 KB (phone 76 KB). Depth maps, lossless WebP: 472 KB (phone 427 KB).

The page (index.html)
  The loop is the background (WebM or H.264), with the AVIF or WebP still as its poster and fallback. A small WebGL
  layer draws the loop through its depth map, pivoted on the stone, so the stone holds still while the near flowers
  and water come forward and the far hall falls back: a slow breath over about a minute and a gentle answer to the
  pointer or tilt. Opening: the hall comes up out of black over about 3.5 s, then the type; any click, key, scroll
  or touch skips it; once per session. Type: the wordmark and "Software, made on purpose." (alternatives in a
  comment), small, lower left. No products, no links.
  The paper: the folded letter leaning against the fallen block is real geometry in the render. A focusable button ("Read the
  note") sits over it; after about seven quiet seconds a soft glint passes over it. Opening it eases the hall in
  towards the letter, the sheet lifts from the block with a turn and settles flat, lit as the hall is (warm from high
  on the left, cool shade on the right) with the fold across its middle and ambientCG Paper002 grain. Escape, the
  close mark or a click outside eases back. Reduced motion: a crossfade, and the still instead of the loop.
  Sound (procedural, muted until the toggle): the hall's air (low filtered noise through a long synthesised
  reverb), birds outside heard through the windows (mostly reverb), and the loop's own two drips into the water,
  timed to the rings. The air swells a little as the pointer moves; the hall goes muffled while the letter is open.
  Phones: the tall crop keeps the stone whole and the letter in view; on very narrow screens the frame is scaled to
  keep both, anchored at the floor, and the dark vault fades in above.
  Checked with Playwright (Chromium, SwiftShader) at 1440x900 and 390x844: no console errors, no overflow; the
  letter opens and closes. Shots in shots/.

Honest limits
  - The letter reads as folded paper and is about six times its old area, but on a phone it is still small, and on a
    390 px screen it sits close to the left edge (the page leaves it a margin and fades the vault in above).
  - The ridges beyond the far arch are faint: the arch reads as a second hall and sky more than a distant country.
  - The crystal is near #94E6FF but a little deeper and greyer than the swatch (about 126, 175, 189 against 148, 230, 255).
  - The petals are small at this distance (2 to 3 px long at 1920): they read as slow, turning flecks, not as petals.
  - The motes, petals and ripples are image effects over the still: the drips do not splash and nothing casts light.
  - The hero ran 45 minutes and stopped at the cap before 400 samples; it is clean after denoising, but the deep shade
    is a little soft.

The push (finished; the first frames it went through are kept as shots/push-first-frame.png and push-second-frame.png)
  1. The stone, hand carved and old, and clean: stone.py opt-in options (defaults unchanged for every other world):
     growth=False (no moss or lichen on the mark; growth only on the ground at its foot), carve (broad, shallow chisel
     scoops in patches at varied angles, a smooth drafted margin along every arris, pick marks on the seam and cavity
     walls; relief only, so it shows where light rakes across), damp_foot (a darker damp band wicked up from the
     ground), chip_size and chip_dots (larger faceted spalls, no row of small bites). Same Break geometry; honed basalt
     held dark (stone_value 0.68) for weight; the render is not lifted or cooled in post.
  2. The crystal: clear glass (IOR 1.544, sky absorption, a faint body tint, no glow). As in a gem photograph, a soft
     card stands behind the stone, seen only through the glass (invisible to the lens, lights nothing), so the crystal
     has light to transmit and its facets read light and dark; its reflection in the water sees the same. MNEE was
     tried and shows nothing from this camera (the sun crosses away from the lens, so refracted light lands behind the
     stone). A projected caustic at the foot was tried and cut: it read as a glow. garden_post.py still --hold k is a
     light crystal hold towards #94E6FF, off by default.
  3. Depth: the far window is now a great open arch into a second hall, roofless and sunlit, its arcade broken down,
     to a far gable with a great open arch; past it three low ridges (Poly Haven coastal_cliff_01, scaled and laid low,
     a step darker) in aerial haze read from the sky's horizon colour. The beam's dust thins low down so it no longer
     veils the arch where the line of sight crosses it.
  4. The letter: a writing sheet (24 by 34 cm) folded once, leaning back against the fallen block at the edge of the
     light, turned so the sun rakes across the fold; warm paper a touch below white, with its shadow on the block.
     Plants are kept clear of it and of the line from the lens. The phone crop (garden_post.py, build_page.py) now
     centres the letter and the stone together, keeping the stone whole. The page's hit area follows the render's
     letter box on rebuild.
  5. The finish: crystal absorption 2.1 times denser with a slightly stronger body tint, so it lands near #94E6FF in
     render (graded median about 126, 175, 189; no post hold); the ridges a step darker again; the letter 29 by 41 cm.
     The data pass is now written linear (Raw) and kept exact as data.npz, so depth for the parallax and the loop's
     occlusion is right (it went through the sRGB curve before). The loop adds six petals falling through the shaft.
     One grade (garden_post.py grade) for the stills, the phone still and both loops.
