Antimony, the mark

The Crystal A.

A hexagon, the shape of antimony's crystal. Inside every hexagon there is already a triangle: join its alternate corners. We strike that triangle through the metal as an A. What is left in the middle, once everything else has been cut away, is the gold.

Construction

A hexagon, the triangle already inside it, and three numbers.

R 50The hexagon's radius. Every point of the mark sits on it or is found from it.
60°The A's legs run from the top corner to the two lower side corners: an equilateral triangle, every angle 60 degrees. Nothing is drawn by eye.
W 9Each strike is 9 units wide, just under a fifth of the radius: wide enough to read at 16 pixels, narrow enough that the metal stays the subject.
¾The bar is struck three quarters of the way down the A, so the counter above it holds nine sixteenths of the triangle.
4Four pieces result: two wings, the counter and the foot. Three triangles and a pentagon.

Motion

Struck three times. What is left turns to gold.

The mark is never drawn on; it is cut out. A solid crystal, the two legs struck, then the bar, then the counter lights. Every reveal, loader and transition in the system is a strike. Tap the mark to strike it again.

The family

Two pieces of the same metal, each struck with its own sign.

Orbe is a block with its orb struck out; Antimony is a crystal with its A struck through. Solid, few parts, cut rather than drawn. Every product after Orbe follows the rule: one block of metal, one strike.

The system

Tiled, the strikes run on from crystal to crystal: the library.

Because the legs run corner to corner, a tiling of marks joins its strikes into one continuous lattice of triangles. It is the pattern for covers, packaging, the website's ground and the motion grid. One counter is lit; it is always the gold.
