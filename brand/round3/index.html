Antimony, the mark, round three

Hopeful, ours, and simple enough to carry for a lifetime.

No emblems, no dark plates, no flowers, stars or orbs. Three marks, each from a true root of the name, each held to the same seven tests, each shown where it will live: a list, an avatar, a lockup, and on skin.

Recommended

The And

Antimony is often read as anti monos: not alone. The oldest sign for not alone is the ampersand, first scratched as a joined e and t on the walls of Pompeii nearly two thousand years ago. Ours is drawn in one continuous line, on the same 45 degree lattice as a crystal, with a single atom held in its upper loop.

  1. An and: never alone, in one character.
  2. The diamond: antimony's crystal, the atom at its heart.
  3. One line: a promise made without lifting the pen.
  4. Two thousand years old as a sign; new as a drawing.
Alternative

The Arch

A lancet arch drawn as an A. Each side is an arc struck from the other side's foot, so neither stands without the other: the way cathedrals stood for eight hundred years. It reads as a doorway, which is the opposite of a fortress.

  1. An A for Antimony.
  2. Two sides that hold each other up.
  3. A door: a way in for the people who make the work.
  4. Built the way things that last are built.
Continuity

The Bond, in line

The molecule from the last round, drawn at one line weight in the way Perplexity draws its mark. Lighter, more precise and less like an emblem than the solid version.

  1. Five electrons around a nucleus.
  2. An A, or a person standing.
  3. A bond: a promise kept.
  4. A lattice that tiles into patterns.
A seed

The And repeats into a pattern; one lit.

Because every stroke sits on the 45 degree lattice, the mark tiles, scales into a grid for layouts, and gives the type its ornaments. The lattice is the system; the And is its smallest sentence.
