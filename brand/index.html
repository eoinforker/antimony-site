Antimony identity, version one

The Bond.

Antimony holds five electrons in its outer shell. Drawn as a molecule, five atoms are bound to one nucleus. Read one way it is an A. Read another, it is a person standing, arms open: the people who make the work. No atom in it stands alone.

Construction

Three numbers make it, on a lattice of squares turned 45 degrees.

D 31The lattice step between atom centres, on a 100 unit square. Every atom sits on it.
R 15.5Each atom, centre to tip. At this size neighbouring tips almost touch, so the bonds read as short and strong rather than as limbs.
W 10The bond, a third of an atom across. Heavy enough to stand beside thick, solid marks; light enough that the diamonds stay the subject.
0Free angles. Every edge runs at 45 or 90 degrees, so the emblem can be rebuilt from this page with a ruler.

Motion

The nucleus first, then the bonds, then the atoms. Then you see it was never alone.

Six seconds. The Bond builds from its nucleus, the camera pulls back to show a lattice of Bonds, the lattice falls away and the name arrives. Square and 16 by 9 versions are in the kit; the website's opening uses the same sequence.

The standard

What it has to clear, and does.

  • Reads in one colour, with no help from cobalt.
  • Reads at 16 px in a browser tab, drawn for that size.
  • Stands as an emblem on its own, like the marks we hold ourselves against, and is built from a true fact about the element rather than a picture of a crystal.
  • Can be rebuilt from three numbers.
  • Moves with a meaning: it builds from the nucleus, and is shown as one of many.
