Antimony standards

Standards.

How Antimony looks, sounds and moves, and why. Written so that anyone who builds with it, now or in fifty years, can make something we would recognise as ours.

A note

Antimony is a metal that was used to refine gold: melted with it, it took up everything that was not gold and left the gold behind. Our mark is a crystal with an A struck through it, and the piece left in the middle is the gold.

This is a standard, not a template. Learn the reasons and you can make anything; copy the examples and you will make the same thing twice. Where these pages give a number, use the number. Where they give a reason, use your judgement.

Five principles
01Struck, not drawnEverything we make is cut from something solid. We reach a design by taking away what it does not need.
02What is left is goldColour is earned. It marks the counter, a link, one lit crystal: the part that matters. Never decoration.
03Never aloneThe mark tiles into a library. Our products share our metal. Our pictures stand on four hundred years of other people's work.
04ExactEvery measure has a reason that can be written down. If a choice cannot be explained, it is not finished.
05Built to lastChoose what will still look right in fifty years: a serif, paper, ink, plain words.
