Cream, stone and ink carry nearly everything. Then one light: sky, the colour of the crystal. Prussian, cobalt and washi support it, and each is used with restraint.

Download swatches

Core colours

The four we reach for first, in every execution. Cream is the ground, stone the mark, ink the night side, and sky the light.

Contrast ratios are measured with the WCAG 2 formula against cream and against ink. Screen values; for print, match each to a drawdown on the stock rather than converting by formula.

Supporting colours

Prussian is sky's partner on paper: sky is a colour you can see but not read on cream, so small things that must be read take Prussian.

Cobalt is loud, kept for a single moment. Washi is a warmer paper for print. None of them should ever outweigh the core.

Sky tones

Sky deepens the way the water in the crystal does: from near white, through the crystal's light, down to Prussian at 800. Sky 300 is the core sky.

Use the tones for depth inside a single piece: a chart, a gradient of light, a set of states. Each tile shows the text colour it takes and its contrast.

Stone tones

The stone runs from cream to stone in nine steps, mixed evenly in the OKLab colour space so each step looks the same distance from the next.

Use them for rules, quiet text, disabled states and the grey of a table. They stay warm without turning brown.

Combinations

These are the pairs we use. Each is a ground and the colour set on it. Pick from these before inventing a new one.

Sky on cream and cream on sky are missing on purpose: they do not have the contrast to carry anything.

Using colour

Start with the coreCream, stone and ink first. Most layouts need nothing else but the crystal.
One lightSky is the only bright colour. Light one thing per layout: the crystal, a link, a figure. If two things are lit, neither is.
Prussian for small thingsOn paper, anything small in our blue is Prussian: the tittle, the counter of the A, a link, a note.
Cobalt, rarelyFor one loud moment, such as a single slide or a sticker. Never as a ground for long text.
NeverNo muddy browns, no amber, no teal, no purple gradients, no glows. No gradient that is not the light in the crystal.
