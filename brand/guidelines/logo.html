About the mark

Antimony is element 51. Its ore, stibnite, grows in long grey crystals, and for centuries it was used to refine gold. Our mark is a piece of that stone, split open.

It reads five ways, and each reading is a part of the drawing: the stone, the seam at sixteen degrees, the cavity cut around the crystal, the crystal, and the light it holds.

Primary lockup

The mark beside the capitals. Use it wherever Antimony is introduced: the website, a letterhead, the first slide, the end of a film.

The capitals stand at 0.22 of the mark's height, centred on it, with 0.34 of the mark between them. Use light colours on dark grounds and dark on light, always.

Stacked lockup

The mark above the capitals, as the website opens. It can stand in for the primary lockup on almost anything: a poster, a card, a cover.

Whenever you use it, centre it on the page or the panel. The capitals run 1.36 times the mark's width, 0.37 of the mark below it.

Symbol

The mark can stand alone wherever the name is already present or there is no room for it: avatars, app icons, favicons, the foot of a page.

We also use the symbol as a design element, drawn in line, huge and thin. See the mark as a design element, below.

Wordmarks

Four wordmarks, each drawn, never typed. The capitals are the primary wordmark. The others are for places where the capitals would be too quiet or too formal.

Use a wordmark alone where the symbol already appears, or where the full lockup would repeat itself: the foot of a document, the last slide, a footer.

Capitals, primary

Timeless Serif capitals, tracked wide and spaced by eye: the A sits closer to the N, the T and I a touch tighter. The A is cut as a crystal; its counter is filled with Prussian on paper and sky on dark grounds.

Lower case

Drawn from Timeless Serif. Three changes make it ours: the tittle of the i is a small crystal, the t joins the i, and the tail of the y is cut on the crystal's angle. For footers, documents and wherever the name is said rather than announced.

Heavy

The lower case thickened by ten units of its 1000. For small sizes, low resolution screens and print below 8 mm high, where the regular drawing would thin out.

Title case

Antimony with the crystal A, for places that read as a sentence: a book spine, a sign off, a title page.

Full colour

Dark stone and a sky crystal. This is the mark we reach for first, on our own channels and wherever colour is possible.

The stone keeps its colour on every ground. On ink and Prussian its cut edges carry a hairline of light, so it never sinks into the ground. On screen, at large sizes, the crystal may hold the moving water, as on the website.

One colour

For one ink: a single colour print, engraving, embossing, a stamp. On light grounds the stone and the crystal take the same ink, and the cavity keeps them apart.

On dark grounds, the one colour version is the line mark. A solid pale tile on a dark ground is never used.

Line mark

The Break drawn in outline: one hairline weight, rounded joins, and nothing filled, so whatever lies behind it shows through.

Draw it in cream on ink or in ink on cream. At small sizes the line is one unit of the mark's 100; drawn large, it stays a single hairline.

Mark as a design element

The line mark can be drawn huge and thin, running off the edges, over our water light, stone, characters or paper. It adds depth without adding things.

Keep the line a hairline and the layout quiet. Use it once per layout, and use the wordmark, not the symbol, anywhere else on the same page.

Construction

Every point is found from a handful of numbers on a square of 100 units. Nothing is placed by eye. The masters in the kit are drawn from these numbers, and so is the website.

Below 30 pixels the mark changes, not just shrinks: the seam widens to 10 and the cavity to 9.2 at the points, so the break still reads at a glance.

100, r 22The tile, and the radius of its corners.
16°The seam's angle from upright. The crystal is sheared to the same angle: each point moves by (51.5 minus y) times tan 16° in x.
50, 51.5The crystal's centre, 1.5 below the middle, so it sits rather than floats.
60 × 38.4The crystal: 60 tall, 38.4 across at the shoulders, which are 25.2 from each point.
7.5 to 3.65The seam: 7.5 wide beside the crystal, thinning to 3.65 where it leaves the stone, and on to a fifth of 7.5 beyond the edge.
7.5 and 4.3The cavity: the crystal grown outward, 7.5 clear at its points and 4.3 at its narrowest.
28%The facet, the crystal's right half, lit with white at 28 per cent.
10, 9.2The small drawing, for 16 to 30 pixels: seam 10 wide beside the crystal, cavity 9.2 clear at the points.

Clear space and size

Keep 22 units clear on every side, the radius of the tile's corner: a little over a fifth of the mark. Nothing enters it. The same measure holds for the symbol, the lockups and the wordmarks.

The smallest sizes, shown below at their true size on screen. Smaller than this, the seam closes up and the capitals blur.

Product lockups

Each product keeps its own mark and its own name. Where its maker needs naming, the product lockup adds by Antimony in Timeless Serif Light, at the product's x-height.

The suffix is Prussian on paper and sky on dark grounds. It says who makes the product, and nothing more. Never join our mark to another company's, and never use a lockup to suggest a partnership.
