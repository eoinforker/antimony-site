Antimony, the mark, the three you chose

The Break, the Seal and the Line, finished.

These are the three you picked out as better than anything since: the tile with the crystal grown in the break, the crystal A in its ring, and the two crystals in one line. Nothing new has been added. Each is finished and tested where wealth and institutions show their marks: pressed into a metal card, cut into stone, as an app icon, beside Orbe, with the name and at 16 pixels. Each has an honest score and an honest list of what it could be mistaken for.

01

The Break

A stone split at sixteen degrees, the seam tapering like a real crack, and a crystal grown in the break. Antimony is what forms where the rock gives way: value found in the break, under pressure.

Built

A square stone with 22 unit corners on a 100 unit grid. The seam runs at 16 degrees and tapers to a point at both edges. The crystal is a long hexagon 38.4 by 60, its shoulders 25.2 down, its right half lit. Below 32 pixels the seam opens by 2.5 units and the cavity by 1.5 so the break still reads.

Could be mistaken for

Deutsche Bank's slash in a square (the closest risk, and the reason it reads as an institution). A lightning bolt, if the crystal is lost. Not a flame, a pin or a drop.

Honest score

8 out of 10. The only one that already reads as a company worth billions at app icon size. It needs a second, quieter form for print and buildings, shown in the one colour and Orbe cut versions.

The Seal

The crystal with the A struck through it, inside a ring: the emblem of a house, made to be stamped, sewn, engraved and worn. Arasaka and Ghost in the Shell, on our side of the table.

Built

A regular hexagon of radius 50, its own triangle struck through as an A with strikes 9 units wide and the bar three quarters down, set inside a ring of 5 units at 72 per cent. Name in tracked capitals beneath for ceremonial use only.

Could be mistaken for

The anarchy sign, an A in a circle. This is a real risk and the main reason it should be a seal for ceremonies rather than the everyday mark. Also a twenty sided die without the ring.

Honest score

7 out of 10 as a primary mark, 9 as a seal. It has the most presence of the three, and the most risk.

03

The Line

Two crystals drawn in one line weight, overlapping on a single axis: two that make one, never alone. The overlap, the lens they share, is lit. Perplexity's discipline, applied to a crystal.

Pressed into a metal card
Cut into stone
antimony
Beside Orbe, with the name, and at 16 to 48 pixels
Built

Two hexagons 40 by 80, shoulders 20 down, their centres 24 apart, on one vertical axis that runs the full height. One stroke weight of 4.4 units; 8 units below 32 pixels. The shared lens is filled in sky.

Could be mistaken for

A gemstone or jewellery brand. Two overlapping shapes can recall a wedding ring motif. Nothing in technology owns it.

Honest score

7 out of 10. The most elegant and the most Perplexity, and the least likely to make you feel something at 16 pixels. Best as the line drawing of the system: diagrams, packaging, fine print.

My recommendation

One family, three jobs.

The Break is the mark: the app icon, the avatar, the product, the thing people will know us by. It is the only one of the three that already reads as a company of real scale at 16 pixels, and it pairs with Orbe because both are solid blocks with something cut from them.

The Seal is the house emblem: for the letters, the certificates, the side of a building, a ring. Kept for ceremony, its risk of reading as the anarchy sign stays small.

The Line is the drawing: the fine line version of the crystal for diagrams, packaging and the moments that should be quiet.

If you agree, I take the Break alone to the level of Perplexity's guidelines next, with the Seal and the Line as its named family members, and drop everything else.
